### Claude:

Quick answer first, then a prototype agent renders both rules side by side.

**Class-first** flips a coin at the walker's node. With chance P it picks the axis class, else the ring class. Then it picks one edge uniformly inside that class. The class gets its chance as a whole, no matter how many edges it holds.

**Degree-weighted** gives every axis edge the weight P and every ring edge the weight 1-P. The walker picks one edge in proportion to its weight. A class with more edges gets more chance.

The two rules agree on an interior node with two axis edges and two ring edges. There the axis class gets exactly P under both rules. They differ where the class sizes are unequal:

| Node | Edges | Class-first, P 0.5 | Degree-weighted, P 0.5 | Uniform sampler today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interior | 2 axis, 2 ring | axis 1/2 | axis 1/2 | axis 1/2 |
| End ruling of a tree segment | 1 axis, 2 ring | axis 1/2 | axis 1/3 | axis 1/3 |
| Patch border | 1 axis, 2 ring | axis 1/2 | axis 1/3 | axis 1/3 |
| Corner | 0 axis, 2 ring | ring (fallback) | ring | ring |

So at 50% degree-weighted is the current sampler, bit for bit in distribution. Class-first is not. It favors the lone axis edge at every segment end and patch border, which is why the tree segments drifted from Wilson in the C1 stats.

There is a second difference under the hood. Degree-weighted is a random walk on edge weights. Origin Shift with such a walk converges to the weighted spanning tree distribution, where a tree's weight is the product of its edge weights. A weighted Wilson's algorithm samples that distribution exactly, so the prototype can check the biased sampler against an exact reference at every P. Class-first is not an edge-weight walk. Its limit distribution has no such form, so Wilson cannot serve as its reference at any P other than 50%, and even there it is off.

In the images the difference is small at 0.1 and 0.9, where both rules run strongly around the ring or along the shaft. At 0.5 class-first shows a few more lengthwise paths at the chunk ends of tree segments. The agent renders both rules so you can compare them directly.
