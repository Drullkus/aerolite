### Claude:

The simple check breaks the paint, because the air writes are what remove blocks that the fill already wrote.

The paint runs in this sequence on the same positions:

1. Fill writes the wall block and the interior block over the whole 13x13 cross section, and the box block over the whole 15x15x15 box.
2. Carve writes air at every maze path surface.
3. Decay writes air at removed walls and paths.
4. Erosion writes air from the surface inward.
5. Shear writes air near the column ends.

With `inChunk(pos) && !state.isAir()` in `set`, step 1 still lands, and steps 2 to 5 land nothing. The result is a solid tube with no maze paths, no decay, no erosion and no shear. The only way to know which block a position ends up with is to let all five steps run, then look. That is what the buffer does: the chunk only ever sees the final block, and only when it is solid.

On cost, the buffer likely saves time rather than costs it. Today a maze path surface takes two `setBlockState` calls, one for the wall and one for the air, and each call on a proto chunk updates heightmaps and section state. With the buffer each position reaches the chunk at most once. The arrays are transient, one `BlockState[4096]` per 16-block band the shaft touches, allocated on first write. A horizontal shaft touches one or two bands. A column touches every band of the build height, about 24 in the Overworld, which is roughly 800 KB of references for the duration of one chunk paint and then garbage.

Two lighter shapes exist if you want them:

- **Restore instead of skip.** Before the first write at a position, remember the block the chunk held there. An air write puts that block back. Same information, same memory, and the chunk takes every intermediate write, so it is slower than the buffer.
- **Decide before the fill.** Build the carve set and the decay set first, then let the fill ask per block whether carve, decay, erosion or shear removes it, and skip the write when it does. No array at all, but it turns the five sequential passes into one pass with four predicates, and the patch rule where decay writes a path block under a removed wall needs its own case. That is a restructure of `ChunkPaint`, not a two-line change.

My reading is that the buffer is the smallest correct change and not a performance risk. If the column memory bothers you, the array can shrink to the bounding box of the shafts in the chunk instead of full bands.
